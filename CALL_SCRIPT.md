# Cold-Call Script — "No Website" Local Businesses

Your edge on every one of these calls: **they already have a great reputation
but no website.** You're not selling them on being good at their job — they
know they're good. You're pointing out that everyone Googling them right now
finds nothing, and their competitors are catching those customers instead.

Keep it short. The goal of the call is **not** to close — it's to book a
10-minute follow-up or get a "yes, send me an example."

---

## The 15-second opener

> "Hi, is this [Owner / the owner]? — Hey, my name's **[YOUR NAME]**, I'm local
> here in Asheville. I'll be quick, I'm not a robot and this isn't a
> telemarketer thing. I was looking for a plumber online and I actually
> couldn't find a website for you guys — but I saw you've got like **[X]
> five-star reviews**, which is incredible. Are you the right person to talk to
> about that?"

Why it works:
- "I'm local / I'll be quick / not a robot" disarms the cold-call reflex.
- Leading with their **reviews** is a genuine compliment — they relax.
- "I couldn't find a website" is the problem, stated as a fact, not a pitch.

---

## The hook (after they engage)

> "Here's the only reason I called. When someone in Asheville Googles 'plumber
> near me,' the businesses with a website show up and get the click — even the
> ones with way worse reviews than you. You've got the reputation, you're just
> invisible at the exact moment someone's ready to call. You're basically
> handing those jobs to your competition without knowing it."

Then **stop talking.** Let them respond.

---

## Discovery — 3 questions, then shut up

1. "Right now, how do most of your new customers find you — word of mouth,
   referrals, anything online?"
2. "Have you ever lost a job because someone couldn't find you or check you out
   online first?"
3. "If I could get you showing up when people search — and looking as good as
   your reviews already are — is that something worth 10 minutes to look at?"

---

## The offer / close (book the next step, don't hard-sell)

> "I'm not going to pitch you on the phone. What I'd like to do is build you a
> quick one-page mockup — your name, your reviews, your phone number, click-to-
> call — so you can actually see it. No cost, no commitment. If you like it, we
> talk. If not, you keep it. Fair?"
>
> "What's the best email or cell to send it to? And when's a better time to
> reach you — mornings or after the workday?"

Get a **specific** next action: a mockup to send, or a calendar time. Vague
"call me sometime" = a no.

---

## Objection handling

| They say… | You say… |
|---|---|
| "I'm too busy / on a job." | "Totally get it — that's exactly why I keep this quick. Give me a better time, even 6pm tonight, and I'll call you then." |
| "I get all my work from word of mouth." | "That's the best kind, and it tells me you're good. This just catches the people who *aren't* referred yet — the ones Googling at 9pm with a burst pipe. Pure upside on top of what you've already got." |
| "I had a website, it didn't do anything." | "Yeah, a lot of them are just brochures that sit there. The difference is making it actually show up in search and turn a Google click into a phone call. Let me show you the difference — no cost." |
| "How much is this?" | "Depends what you need, and I'm not going to quote you blind. That's why I want to build the free mockup first — then you'll know exactly what you're paying for. Worst case you get a free preview." |
| "Send me some info." | "Happy to — but generic info is useless. Give me 10 minutes and I'll send you a mockup with *your* business on it, so it's actually relevant. What's the best email?" |
| "Not interested." | "No problem, I appreciate you. Can I at least text you the mockup once it's done? If it's not for you, just ignore it." |

---

## Voicemail (keep under 20 seconds)

> "Hey, this message is for the owner — my name's **[YOUR NAME]**, I'm local in
> Asheville. I was trying to find your website and couldn't, but your reviews
> are fantastic, so I wanted to reach out. I've got a quick idea that could get
> you more calls — no pitch, takes two minutes. Give me a ring back at
> **[YOUR NUMBER]**. Thanks, talk soon."

Then **text the same thing** — texts get read, voicemails don't.

### Calling tips
- **Best windows for trades:** 7–8am before they head out, or 4–6pm as they wrap
  up. Avoid mid-morning (they're on jobs).
- Smile while you talk — it carries.
- One call, one goal: the next step. Don't try to win the whole deal on call #1.
- Log every call (answered / VM / callback time) so you can follow up — most
  sales happen on contact #2–#5, not #1.
